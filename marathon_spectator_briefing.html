02 / Premise Why we're doing this walkthrough
First, the honest bit

Having people on the course is the best thing you can do for a runner.

Genuinely. We're grateful you're supporting us through this!

Preparation is key

From the start pen to the finish line, we're unreachable.

Phones famously don't work for either spectators or runners. The tracker app might not be accurate. I won't know where you are, and you won't reliably know where I am. This is just how marathons work.

The one ask

We'll need you to organise yourselves. Pick your spots on the google sheet, split into groups if you like. Preferably, do it before race day, not on it. The rest of this briefing is just giving you some tips to do that.

03 / Reality Check What will actually go wrong

Four things will misbehave.

Don't panic on the day, this is just how these things go.

01

Mobile signal

Phones will mostly be out or unreliable. There's jsut too many people. Expect drops, delays, and messages that arrive twenty minutes late or not at all.

02

The official app

The TCS London Marathon app tracks runners via timing mats every 5 km. Between mats, you see nothing. The app is good but it is also subject to the same connectivity issues. Useful, but don't trust it alone.

03

Our pace

We might be faster, but most likely slower than planned. A 30-minute delay is realistic.

04

Crowds

At good spots, crowds are deep. Normally people arrive to the spectating place a bit earlier, queue and as people see their runners they move and you'll get a chance to see us.

07 / Self-Organising The bit that needs the most thought

You may choose to be all together but if you're thinking of splitting in groups read here. Use the whatsapp group to coordinate, share locations, and ask for help on the day.

A sensible split looks like:

  • Group A — Early course (Mile 6–13). Catches us fresh, commits to one spot, accepts they won't see us again until the finish area.
  • Group B — Late course (Mile 19–23). Lies in until mid-morning, catches us where the support matters most, walks to the finish area after.
  • Optional Group C — Cana Water miles 9 and 11. One spot, two sightings, highest-return-for-effort.

Each group needs, before race day:

  • An agreed spot, confirmed by looking at the course map
  • A tube plan to get there and an arrival time (aim to be in place 60–90 min before the expected runner window)
  • Location-sharing turned on between group members
  • Think of lunch, fallback plans etc
08 / Afterwards How we find each other

The finish area might be tricky.

It's chaos. Fences, baggage trucks, tens of thousands of tired runners, family reunion zones organised by surname letter. Don't agree to "just meet at the finish."

Process

What happens to me after I cross

I collect my medal, baggage, food, and walk very slowly through the finish funnel. Realistically that's 60ish minutes before I'm out and mobile. I'll be on my phone once I have baggage back.

Reality check

We will be too tired to think clearly. Please don't plan anything that requires us to be sharp, walk far, or socialise with large groups in the first hour after finishing. A seat, food, and a quiet room is roughly the ceiling of what I can handle.

Recommended post-race meeting points:

  • Family meet area at Horse Guards Parade, which is organised by runner surname, official, but crowded
  • We added in the previous guide an option for a photo place+a burger spot!
09 / Closing If you remember nothing else
The whole briefing in five lines

Five things.
That's it.

  1. 01

    Install the app, add our bibs.

  2. 02

    Turn on location sharing with each other.

  3. 03

    Split into 2–3 groups. Pick spots. Pick a group lead.

  4. 04

    Agree a meet-up point that is NOT the finish line.

  5. 05

    Do all of this before race day.

Seeing familiar faces on the course is genuinely a big deal. Thank you for doing this. Now go organise yourselves.
