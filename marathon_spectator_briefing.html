02 / Premise Why we're doing this walkthrough
First, the honest bit

Having people on the course is the best thing you can do for a runner.

Genuinely. I'm grateful you're giving up a Sunday for this.

Now, the less fun bit

From the start pen to the finish line, I'm unreachable.

I won't have a phone. The tracker app will lie. Signal will be dodgy. I won't know where you are, and you won't reliably know where I am. This is just how marathons work — it isn't me being difficult.

The one ask

Organise yourselves. Pick your spots, split into groups, agree a meet-up point for after. Do it before race day, not on it. The rest of this briefing is just giving you what you need to do that.

03 / Reality Check What will actually go wrong

Four things will misbehave.

I'm telling you now so you don't panic on the day.

01

Mobile signal

Half a million people in central London, mostly on the same networks, mostly with phones out. Expect drops, delays, and messages that arrive twenty minutes late or not at all.

02

The official app

The TCS London Marathon app tracks runners via timing mats every 5 km. Between mats, you see nothing. The app also crashes, lags, and occasionally reports nonsense. Useful, but don't trust it alone.

03

Our pace

We might be faster than planned. We might be slower. A 15-minute window either way is realistic. If you turn up expecting me at a precise time, you'll miss me.

04

Crowds

At good spots, crowds are 4–6 deep. Arrive early or you're watching the backs of other people's heads. Pick your spot deliberately, not "wherever".

Peer-to-peer

Apple Find My (iPhone users)

Share your location with each other before race day. This is how you find each other in crowds when signal is patchy — it syncs opportunistically rather than needing a live connection every second.

Android users: Google Maps location sharing does the same job. Set it up in the same way, same group.

06 / Geography Where to actually stand

Here's the plan.
Three cheer points.

These are the specific spots we've scouted. Gap between point 2 and point 3 is 2+ hours — plenty of time to eat, rest, or explore.

Cheer point 1 · Early race

Mile 9

Very close to Canada Water station. Gets busy, but there's a corner that offers a good view of the runners.

Cheer point 2 · Approaching Tower Bridge

Mile 11

Close to you, less crowded than Tower Bridge itself. After this, you're free to eat, explore, or rest until the final stretch.

Cheer point 3 · Late race

Just after Blackfriars underpass ~mile 24

Recommended last cheer point. Runners emerge from the underpass here. Nearest station: London Blackfriars (rail).

Finish area · Only if skipping Blackfriars

Horse Guards Parade

Post-race meeting points are alphabetically signposted along Horse Guards Road. Our letter is TBD — we'll share once we have it from the race pack.

Pick one: Blackfriars OR the finish

If you cheer at Blackfriars (mile 24), getting to Horse Guards in time is extremely difficult. Road closures and crowds make it nearly impossible to move fast enough. We tried spectating last year and couldn't make it. Choose the one that matters more to you.

07 / Self-Organising The bit that needs the most thought

Don't be one big blob.
Be two or three groups.

One big group tries to do everything together, fails, and ends up seeing the runners zero times. Split up.

A sensible split looks like:

  • Group A — Early course (Mile 6–13). Catches us fresh, commits to one spot, accepts they won't see us again until the finish area.
  • Group B — Late course (Mile 19–23). Lies in until mid-morning, catches us where the support matters most, walks to the finish area after.
  • Optional Group C — Canary Wharf double-back. One spot, two sightings, highest-return-for-effort.

Each group needs, before race day:

  • A designated group lead (one person with the sheet open and the app installed)
  • An agreed spot, confirmed by looking at the course map
  • A tube plan to get there and an arrival time (aim to be in place 60–90 min before the expected runner window)
  • Location-sharing turned on between group members
  • A shared WhatsApp or similar — don't try to ad-hoc it on the day
08 / Afterwards How we find each other once I've stopped running

The finish area
is not the meet-up spot.

It's chaos. Fences, baggage trucks, tens of thousands of tired runners, family reunion zones organised by surname letter. Don't agree to "just meet at the finish."

Process

What happens to me after I cross

I collect my medal, baggage, food, and walk very slowly through the finish funnel. Realistically that's 30–60 minutes before I'm out and mobile. I'll be on my phone once I have baggage back.

Decision

Pick a real meet-up spot

A pub, a café, a specific street corner — somewhere away from the finish congestion but within walking distance. Agree it before race day. Share the pin in the group chat.

Reality check

I will be wrecked. Don't plan anything that requires me to be sharp, walk far, or socialise with large groups in the first hour after finishing. A seat, food, and a quiet room is roughly the ceiling of what I can handle.
