Track & Core Nutrition

General guidance, not medical advice. If you have a medical condition or a special diet, ask a professional.

Around your runs

Draft: captains to confirm

2 hours before
Carbohydrate that is easy to digest, e.g. a white bagel with jam, toast, banana and honey, pancakes or porridge.
Runs up to 65 min
In the morning, you can go without food.
Runs over 65 min
Eat before you go.
Runs over 2 hours
Fuel during the run. See gels.
After
After a run of more than 90 minutes or a speed session: a drink or snack with about 3 parts carbohydrate to 1 part protein.

Every day: low glycaemic index

These foods release their energy slowly. Make them the base of your meals.

Bread
Wholemeal, multigrain, rye, sourdough
Breakfast
Porridge with rolled oats, muesli, All-Bran
Fruit
Apples, strawberries, apricots, peaches, plums, pears, kiwi
Vegetables
Carrots, broccoli, cauliflower, celery, tomatoes
Starchy vegetables
Orange sweet potato, corn, yams, Carisma and Nicola potatoes
Legumes
Lentils, chickpeas, baked beans, butter beans, kidney beans
Pasta and noodles
Pasta, soba, vermicelli, rice noodles
Rice
Basmati, Doongara, long-grain, brown
Grains
Quinoa, barley, pearl couscous, buckwheat

Race week and race day

Draft: captains to confirm

2–3 days before
Eat a little more carbohydrate at each meal, and add two or three snacks. A common target is 7–12 g of carbohydrate per kg of body weight per day. Less fibre and fat than usual. Only foods that you know.
Race morning
Breakfast 2–3 hours before the start, one that you have tested before a long run.
During
30–60 g of carbohydrate per hour. Start early. See gels.
After
Carbohydrate and protein within about an hour, fluid, then a proper meal. Eat well for a few days.

Nothing new on race day. Test each food, gel and drink on your long runs first.
