Track & Core FAQs

General guidance, not medical advice. Your question is not here? Ask a coach.

Gels

Do I need gels?

Draft: captains to confirm

For the marathon, yes, or a different source of carbohydrate. Your body stores enough for about 90–120 minutes of marathon effort.

In training, fuel during runs over 2 hours. Your long runs are the time to practise.

What types of gel are there?

Draft: captains to confirm

Standard
20–25 g of carbohydrate. Take it with water.
Isotonic
Thinner, no water necessary, less carbohydrate in each.
Hydrogel
Like jelly, easier on the stomach, more expensive.
High-carb
30–40 g or more. Can be heavy on the stomach.
Caffeine
Often 30–100 mg of caffeine. Test it in training.
Real food
Honey or fruit gels, chews, jelly sweets, dates, banana.

Try many, and use the ones that your stomach accepts. Read the grams of carbohydrate on the label.

How much, and when?

Draft: captains to confirm

30–60 g of carbohydrate per hour. For most gels, that is one every 25–40 minutes. In the race, take the first one after 30–45 minutes. Do not wait until you are tired.

Water or sports drink with a gel?

Draft: captains to confirm

Water, a few mouthfuls. A gel with a sports drink can be too much sugar at once. Isotonic gels do not need water.

When do I use caffeine gels?

Draft: captains to confirm

Most runners keep them for the second half of the race. Caffeine is different for each person, so test it on a long run.

Do I train with the gels of the race?

Draft: captains to confirm

Yes, and with your own. If you drop a gel or need more, you can then use one from the course.

In recent years: Lucozade Sport Energy Gel at London, and HIGH5 Energy Gel Aqua at Brighton. Sponsors change: read the 2027 event guide.

How do I prevent stomach problems?

Draft: captains to confirm

Practise on your long runs. Start with a small quantity. Take a gel in sips, not all at once. In race week, eat less fibre and fat than usual.

Hydration

How much do I drink in the race?

Draft: captains to confirm

About 300–600 ml per hour in normal weather, and more in heat. Drink when you are thirsty, not at every station: far too much is dangerous. If you sweat a lot, you can need electrolytes.

Gait analysis

What is it, and do I need it?

Draft: captains to confirm

In a shop, it is a short video of your feet on a treadmill. It helps you to choose a shoe. There is little evidence that it prevents injury.

  • No injury, and happy with your shoes? You do not need it.
  • First running shoes? A shop fitting is a good idea.
  • Pain that comes back? See a physio or a podiatrist.

Training

What finish time is my target?

None. Your first marathon is your biggest PB. Start easy, enjoy the day and finish with a smile.

I missed a week. What now?

Draft: captains to confirm

Do not try to add the missed runs. Continue with the plan where it is now. If you were ill or missed more than a week, repeat your last full week first. With a fever or chest symptoms, rest until you are well.

Can I do parkrun the day before a long run?

Yes, as an easy jog or as a volunteer. Do not race it. Before the longest run, rest or keep it very gentle.

What is a deload week?

A week with a shorter long run, after some weeks of building. Your body uses it to absorb the training. It is not a week off.

I feel slow in the taper. Is that normal?

Yes. Heavy legs and doubt are common before the race. Trust the taper, and do not add miles.

How fast are my long runs?

Use effort, not pace: about 6/10. If you cannot talk, slow down. Walk breaks are fine, also on race day.
